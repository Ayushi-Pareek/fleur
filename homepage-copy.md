## 2. Why Botanical Paper Sculpture

### Made to last
No water, no wilting, no weekly replacements. With basic care, a paper sculpture holds its colour and shape for years — often decades.

### In season, all year
Roses in February. Hellebores in August. If a flower belongs in your room, it doesn't have to be in season.

### One of a kind
Every leaf is cut and shaped by hand from the studio's own studies of the plant, so no two pieces are identical. Each one is signed and dated by the artist. Pieces are released in small batches; when a batch sells out, the studio makes more in its own time. (Never "one of one" — the site does not claim pieces are single editions.)

### The easier bouquet
Nothing to order, trim, or throw away at the end of the week. Fresh arrangements are a repeat purchase — a paper sculpture simply stays.

## The honest-process note (living study correction)

The site no longer claims works are made "from living study" or "from life." The truthful process, which is also a better story: **every piece begins with the plant** — held, taken apart, and photographed petal by petal — because a sculpture can take two weeks and a rose lasts four days. The studio makes its own references (studies, photographs, notes) that outlast the bloom, and works from those. Where the copy references process, it says "the studio's own studies of the plant" or "studied and photographed at the worktable." Nothing claims the fresh stem survives the build; nothing claims photographs aren't used.

## Provenance note (the Pune question)

The homepage deliberately omits the city. "Shaped by hand, from life" carries the provenance; the Pune studio story belongs on the About page, told properly — the worktable, the garden, the study stems — where narrative context absorbs geography instead of anchoring price against it.

Rationale:
- **Pay-parity anchoring is real, mostly digital.** Location-based price discounting hits templates and courses hardest. Those sections already carry no location, and their pricing is set by depth of instruction, not geography.
- **The physical objects defend themselves.** Materials lineage (crepe, pigments), process detail, editions, and signed-and-dated works are what collectors actually price. A city name adds nothing to that equation.
- **This is standard practice, not concealment.** Non-Western luxury brands routinely lead with the maker's name and let location surface in the story. Nothing on the site claims otherwise; the About page says Pune plainly and proudly.

If Ayushi later wants the city on the homepage, the placement that reads best is the About link and artist signature — never the hero.

## Product voice (for collection and product pages)

- **Name works species-first, like botanical plates.** "Rose No. 3," "Hellebore, Dark Stem." The plant is the subject; colour and edition are qualifiers. Never "Pink Rose Art Piece."
- **Tiered architecture — collect / learn / make.** The site runs three clearly separated tiers:
  1. **Art (collect).** Sculptures and commissions. One-of-a-kind language — handmade, no two identical, signed and dated — **never "one of one"**: multiple units of a plant may exist, released in small batches that sell out and are made again. Flags read "Sold out." "Add to collection" CTA. Shipping is worldwide, tracked. The only tier shown in the shop.
  2. **The Fleur Method (learn).** Premium teaching of the *real* technique — recorded classes, in-person workshops, the stem kit. Framed as authority, never as the accessible alternative. Never talks down to the craft tier.
  3. **Print & Make (make).** "Make something this afternoon" — the artist's wind-down practice and experiments shelf. Own label, own tone, openly credited to the studio.
- **Price proximity rule.** No maker price (templates/classes/kits) ever appears on the homepage, the shop, or any art page — maker prices live only on `method.html` and `printables.html`. The shop carries a single doorway line ("Learn the Technique").
- **Links between maker tiers are time-framed, both ways:** The Method → Print & Make ("Short on time? Make something in an afternoon"); Print & Make → The Method ("When an afternoon stops being enough"). Never quality-framed ("easier", "simplified") in either direction. The art pages never mention either beyond the shop's single doorway line.
- **Suggested price ladder:** printable patterns ($18) → stem kit ($42) → recorded classes ($145) → workshops → core sculptures ($195–$310) → statement work ($980). Each tier feeds the one above.
