## Homepage section mapping

1. **Hero** — Paper ground, Ink text, eyebrow in Moss, primary CTA in Pine (Paper text), hover to Clay.
2. **Why blocks** — Paper continues; titles in Pine; keep the four blocks airy, no panel color.
3. **The Rose Series** — the art carries color; photography on Sage-wash or dark Basalt ground for gallery weight. Ivory captions.
4. **Commissions** — the dark section: Basalt or Umber ground, Paper text, Petal accent on the button. One dramatic inversion per page.
5. **Learn the Technique** — Stone/Oat panel; Clay accents. The one section where warm clay tones do the talking — signals workshop without going crafty.
6. **Studio Journal** — Paper ground, links in Fog or Moss, meta text in Mushroom.
7. **Collector's List** — return to Paper with a Pine or Clay button; end quiet, not loud.

## Usage rules

- **Ink, never black.** All text sits in #404038 or darker palette tones.
- **Clay is the accent of record.** Buttons, hovers, small marks. Petal is its quiet sibling — highlights, "new" tags, underlines.
- **Botanical greens structure, not decorate.** Moss/Pine for eyebrows, titles, CTA grounds; Sage only at large sizes (fails contrast for small text on Paper, ~1.9:1).
- **Fog is rare.** One cool note in a warm system — links, a single line per page at most.
- **Dark sections: max one per page.** Basalt/Umber grounds give the gallery moment; two inverts the airiness the brand depends on.
- Contrast checks: Ink on Paper ≈ 9:1 (AA/AAA). Paper on Pine ≈ 9:1. White on Clay ≈ 3.3:1 — large text only; prefer Paper-on-Pine for CTAs.

## Why it fits

The palette already *is* the copy's register — English-country naturalist, nothing bright, nothing pure. Terracotta + dusty rose echo the roses themselves; the four botanical greens do the provenance work; ivory ground keeps the whole thing a sheet of good paper. The brand doesn't need a color the products don't contain.
