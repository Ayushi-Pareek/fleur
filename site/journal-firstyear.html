Living With a Piece: The First Year

Ayushi · 3 min read

Collectors write, sometimes, at the one-year mark — and the letters are oddly consistent. The piece has settled into the room the way furniture does, only better, because furniture doesn't nod. Here is what the first year is actually like, gathered from those letters and from the studio's own pieces that live at home.

The first month is admiring. You look at it often, from the doorway, on the way past — the piece is still slightly an event. Somewhere in the second or third month it becomes furniture's equal: part of the room, missed when dusted around, greeted after holidays away. That transition is the point. A sculpture should end up lived-with, not under glass.

What changes in a year: almost nothing, if the two rules are kept. The colour holds — the dilute dye layers go deep enough to outlast a decade of ambient light. The posture holds; the wire does not tire of the pose it was set in. What changes is you. Nearly every collector reports the same small discovery: they notice light differently now, in rooms that have no sculpture in them.

The one thing never to do: put it where water can reach it. Not sunlight, not drafts — water. Steam is the whole enemy.

Care, in practice, turns out to be almost nothing: a breath of cool air from a hairdryer held back, two or three times a year. No water, no wipes, no sprays. The piece asks for less than any living thing in the house, and gives back more than most.

One practical note

If you keep a piece at home: dust it with cool air — the cool setting of a hairdryer, held back — and keep it out of direct sun and damp. That is the entire care instruction. The rest is looking.
