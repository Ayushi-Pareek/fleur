About

The studio of Ayushi

Ayushi makes flowers the way they were always going to end up: still here.

Every piece begins with the plant. A stem comes in from the garden or the market and sits on the worktable in a jar — held, drawn, measured, and taken apart petal by petal, with photographs and notes taken at every stage. The photographs matter: a sculpture can take two weeks to build, and a rose lasts four days on the table. So the studio makes its own references — studies and photographs made to outlast the bloom — and the flower can be rebuilt, truthfully, long after the flower itself is gone.

The crepe is fine-weight and dyed by hand in small batches, because no living flower is one flat colour. The wire armature underneath is what gives each leaf its posture: the droop, the reach, the unfurling that is half-finished on purpose.

Every piece stands as the plant would — potted, stem in soil — and is signed and dated on the underside, like a drawing. When a work is finished it is photographed once, from the angle the study was made at, and released. Most pieces find their rooms within weeks; some are commissioned before they exist.

The studio is in Pune, India, where the growing season is generous and the winter light — the best light for looking at a leaf — lasts exactly as long as the hellebore studies take to build.

What the studio believes

The plant comes first

Every work starts with the real thing — held, taken apart, and photographed petal by petal. Because blooms fade before a piece is finished, those photographs and studies become the true reference. Nothing is invented from imagination.

Materials that outlast the trend

Fine crepe, wire, wood, and terracotta. Nothing that dates the piece to a moment, nothing that asks to be replaced.

one of a kind. Handmade, signed and dated.

Each sculpture is made by hand, petal by petal — so no two are ever identical, even of the same plant. Pieces are released in small numbers, signed and dated; when a batch is gone, the studio makes more, and each remains its own.
