Why this experiment

Notes from the worktable

This experiment came out of the journals themselves: a junk journal wants paper that looks lived-with, and new printer paper refuses to. So began a long, pleasant series of tests — coffee against tea, oven-drying against air-drying, crumpling before dyeing and after — to find out which method makes paper look old rather than merely stained.

What was learned: tea gives a soft grey-tan and coffee gives a deep brown, blotching is the beauty (chase it, don't fight it), and edges dried with a hair-dryer curl in a way that looks convincingly like time. What's next: a full journal kit — spine, pockets, and tags to match. The first dyed pages went into the artist's own junk journal, which is where every experiment ends up eventually.
