How a Rose Travels

Ayushi · 2 min read

A piece that has survived two weeks of careful making deserves a better ending than a courier bin. So every sculpture leaves the studio the same way: packed as it stands, in a box made for it alone.

The box is built to the piece, not the other way round — measured against the stem's actual lean, the bloom's actual width, so nothing floats and nothing presses. The stem is secured at the angle it was made to hold; the bloom sits in a cradle of soft paper, unable to shift. From the moment the lid closes, the sculpture is held exactly as it would stand in your room.

Then it travels — worldwide, tracked, from the studio door to yours. Duties and taxes, where they apply, are set by your country and payable on delivery; everything else is arranged on this end. Within India, larger pieces can be delivered and placed in the room by the studio itself.

The box is the last piece of making. It gets the same attention as the first petal.

What arrives at your door: the sculpture, upright and unbothered; a signed and dated card; and a care card with the only instruction the piece will ever need. Unpack slowly. It has been standing still for days — it will stand for years.

One practical note

If you keep a piece at home: dust it with cool air — the cool setting of a hairdryer, held back — and keep it out of direct sun and damp. That is the entire care instruction. The rest is looking.
